## 0.1 Cross-scope flags that change the marketing (read first)

These come from the primary text of Ley 643 de 2001 (#39) [P]:
- **Art. 27, prize type.** A rifa is a draw of premios en especie on a set date, among holders of tickets in continuous series sold at a fixed price by a previously authorized operator. Permanent raffles are banned.
  - A COP 2,000,000 cash prize, a second raffle or weekly mini-draws may not fit. Get a legal check [U].
- **Art. 28, jurisdiction.** One municipality means the municipality authorizes. Two or more municipalities of one department means the department. Two or more departments means the national entity (ETESA in the 2001 text).
  - WhatsApp sales to buyers in other towns could change who must authorize [U].
- **Art. 30, cost.** Exploitation rights are 14% of gross income. They are accredited at authorization on 100% of the issued tickets, then adjusted after the draw [P]. I use this in the pricing table; whether it applies to Santiago's raffle is [U].
- **Art. 4, minors and wording.** It bans offering games of chance to minors, and offers that disguise their random nature or risks [P]. All copy below carries 18+ and "no hay garantía" lines for that reason.
- **Enforcement:**
  - Coljuegos fined an influencer COP 116 million (100 SMMLV of 2023) plus a five-year ban for an unauthorized raffle (Aug 2026, subject to appeal) (#49) [S].
  - It asked Meta to block 289 profiles in Dec 2024 (#48) [S].
  - Municipalities run their own authorization procedure; Acacías lists 8 business days at the Secretaría de Gobierno (#51) [S].
  - A search summary of municipal pages mentioned filing at least 45 days before the draw. That is unverified for Fusagasugá [U].
- **Consequences for marketing:** Don't sell tickets before authorization. Print the resolution number on every piece. The permit sets the timeline, not the calendar [U].

## A. Behavioral science and pricing

### A1. Mechanisms

| Mechanism | What the evidence says | Implication for this raffle [U] |
|---|---|---|
| Probability weighting | Decision weights are generally lower than probabilities except at low probabilities. Overweighting low probabilities may help explain both insurance and gambling [P](#1). Cumulative prospect theory adds diminishing sensitivity and loss aversion [P](#2). A lecture deck reports weighting parameters 0.61 (gains) and 0.69 (losses) [S](#3). With 0.61, I compute a decision weight of about 1.4% for a 0.1% chance (roughly 14x). That extrapolates beyond the probability range the parameters were fitted to [U]. | A 1-in-1,000 chance feels bigger than it is. Say it plainly ("1 en 1.000"). It is at least 10x better than matching the 4-digit lottery number the draw uses [U]. |
| Skewness (long-shot) preference | Bettors' behavior is at least as consistent with risk aversion as risk loving once skewness is considered. They seem to crave skewness [P](#4). The same pattern was reported for US lottery games (I saw only the bibliographic record) [S](#5). | Headline one big, simple prize. |
| Illusion of control, chosen tickets | In Langer's office lottery (53 workers, $1 tickets), choosers wanted $8.67 on average to sell, versus $1.96 for assigned tickets (t=4.33) [P](#6,#8). In Study 6, a 3-digit ticket delivered one digit per day for 3 days raised confidence (6.45 vs 3.00 on 10 points) and keep rates (63.6% vs 31.6%). Cells were about 20 each [P](#6). A later program of 17 experiments (N=10,825) finds choice rarely creates an illusion of control. Where it appears, it reflects pre-existing beliefs that the options differ [P, working paper](#7). | Let buyers pick numbers because people value a number they like. Don't claim that picking raises the odds. |
| Ticket stickiness and regret in swapping | Fewer than 50% of students swapped a lottery ticket for another plus a small bonus, versus over 90% for pens. The authors attribute this to anticipated ex-post regret [P](#8). In a Warsaw field study (n=472), about 85% kept their initial pick despite bonuses of 20–40% of ticket value. 70% preferred random-looking tickets to patterned ones [P](#9). | Expect no swaps. State a "no cambios" rule in the bases. |
| Anticipated regret | Four studies on the Dutch Postcode Lottery, including 100 street interviews. Anticipated regret about not playing when neighbors win predicted intention to play [P/S](#10). | Regret is strongest when winners are near. Show buyer initials on the board, with consent. Keep it playful and never threaten ("te vas a arrepentir"). |
| Near-miss | Near-misses felt less pleasant but raised desire to play, only when players had personal control over arranging the gamble [S](#13). | Near-misses are built into a 3-digit draw. Don't market "casi ganaste". Prizes for adjacent numbers (official lotteries call them aproximaciones [S](#52)) exploit the same effect. |
| Gambler's fallacy and number choice | In a 3-digit-style numbers game, money bet on a number fell sharply right after it was drawn and recovered over months [P](#11). Danish lotto players' switching fits the law of small numbers [S](#12). In Israeli lottery data, 7 was the most-picked number in all 118 draws (4.19% of picks), then 8 (4.05%) [S](#14). Colombian press cites numerology (5, 7, 8; birth and wedding dates; 1234), not data [S](#15). I found no Colombian raffle number-choice data [U]. | Expect "pretty" numbers (007, 777, 123, dates) to sell first. Expect the previous official draw's digits to sell slower. Log sales order to test this. |
| Scarcity | A meta-analysis (416 effect sizes, 131 studies) finds scarcity cues raise purchase intentions, d=0.31 (k=193, CI .25–.37). By type: supply-based .39, time-based .34, demand-based .28. Mostly intentions, lab and scenario data [P](#16). | A live "quedan X de 1.000" counter is the honest version. Scam guides list "artificial urgency" as a red flag [S](#50). |
| Social proof | Hotel towel signs citing most guests' behavior: 44.1% reuse versus 35.1% for an environmental appeal [S](#17). | "Ya van 430 números" works as proof and scarcity. Name known buyers, with consent. |
| Deadlines and the campaign curve | Kickstarter backing is U-shaped: more in the first and last week, across successful and failed projects. Early family-and-friends support matters [P](#18). Support rises as the goal nears, more so near the deadline, for small goals and when early support is thin, and falls after the goal is met [S](#18). Kickstarter is not a raffle [U]. | Front-load through the close network. Plan a mid-campaign relaunch and a hard sales close. Sell-out is the goal line. |
| Endowment | Mug owners demanded about twice what non-owners would pay. Later work disputes how general this is [S](#35). | A held number feels owned. Short holds (about 30 minutes) convert. |
| Small-amount framing | Recasting a cost as small ongoing expenses changes the comparison standard and compliance [P](#19). | Use comparisons sparingly ("un tinto y un pan"). The original effect is about recurring costs, so the fit to a one-off COP 6,000 is partial [U]. |
| Pain of paying | Prepaying reduces felt pain of paying [S](#23). Less transparent payment forms (cards, gift certificates) are spent more freely than cash [S](#23). | At COP 6,000, friction matters more than pain. Let people pay at the moment of "yes" (Bre-B/Nequi QR). Give cash payers a numbered receipt. |

### A2. Fundraising-lottery evidence

| Study | Design | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Morgan 2000 [P](#24) | Theory | Lottery-financed provision exceeds voluntary giving and nears first-best as prizes grow. |
| Morgan & Sefton 2000 [P](#25) | Lab | Provision is higher under lotteries. Ticket purchases respond to prize size, with larger prizes more effective. |
| Dale 2004 [P](#28) | Lab | A fixed-prize lottery draws more participation and funding than a revenue-dependent prize. |
| Landry, Lange, List, Price & Rupp (QJE 2006; figures from the 2005 NBER working paper) [P](#26) | Door-to-door field experiment, 4,833 households. Arms: plain ask, ask with seed money, single $1,000 prize, four $250 prizes; $1 = 1 ticket. | Of households that answered the door, 25.3% gave in the plain ask, 14.8% with seed money, 45.5% in the single-prize lottery and 35.9% in the multi-prize lottery. Average gift per answering household: $1.01, $1.16, $1.89 and $1.52. Lotteries raised about 50% more gross and about 100% more participation. The single-prize lottery would need about 3,030 households per arm to net more than the plain ask after prize cost. A one-SD rise in female solicitor attractiveness changed gifts by an amount similar to lottery vs plain ask. |
| Lange, List & Price 2007 [P](#27) | Lab: plain ask vs single vs multiple prizes | Both lotteries beat the plain ask on average gifts and participation. Total provision was largest with a single prize. Multiple prizes win only with enough risk aversion or asymmetric valuations (theory). |
| Carpenter, Holmes & Matthews 2008 [P](#29) | Field auctions (first-price, second-price, all-pay) | First-price beat the other two. Differential participation explains gaps from theory. |
| DellaVigna, List & Malmendier 2012 [S](#31) | Door-to-door with flyers and an opt-out box | The flyer cut door-opening 9–25%. The opt-out box cut giving 28–42%, mostly gifts under $10. About half of donors would rather not be asked. |
| Castillo, Petrie & Wardell 2014 [S](#32) | Online peer-to-peer asks | Donors respond to incentives. Nuisance costs matter. |
| Gneezy & Rustichini 2000 [P](#33) | 180 high-school students collecting door to door | Average collected per pair: no pay 238.6, 1% commission 153.6, 10% commission 219.3. |

### A3. Single vs several prizes; price, prize, odds; bundles

- **Single vs several prizes.** The Landry field data and the Lange et al. lab data both favor one large prize over splitting the same total into four [P](#26,#27). Both are charity settings [U].
- **Prize size.** Prize size drives ticket purchases (#25) [P]. Lotto sales rise non-linearly with jackpot size (#30) [S]. Effects at COP 2 million scale are unknown [U].
- **Price.** I found no price elasticity data for informal raffles [U].
- **Quantity anchors.** Wansink, Kent & Hoch found that multiple-unit pricing, purchase limits and suggestive selling raised purchase quantities in two field and two lab studies (#20) [S, snippet].
  - Its first author was later found by Cornell to have committed misconduct on other work, with at least 23 retractions (#20) [S].
  - I saw no source naming the 1998 paper among them [U]. Treat the effect sizes with caution.
- **Decoys.** 19 studies with realistic stimuli failed to show a decoy effect (#22) [S].
- **Round prices.** The original rounded-price paper reported five studies (#21) [S]. A preregistered replication (N=588) found no interaction (#21) [P]. Choose prices for change, mental math and QR payments, not for supposed psychology [U].

### A4. Pricing worksheet (my arithmetic [U], using Art. 30's 14% from #39 [P])

| Price / sold | Gross | 14% rights | Net after COP 2,000,000 prize |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6,000 / 50% | 3,000,000 | 420,000 | 580,000 |
| 6,000 / 70% | 4,200,000 | 588,000 | 1,612,000 |
| 6,000 / 90% | 5,400,000 | 756,000 | 2,644,000 |
| 6,000 / 100% | 6,000,000 | 840,000 | 3,160,000 |
| 5,000 / 50% | 2,500,000 | 350,000 | 150,000 |
| 5,000 / 70% | 3,500,000 | 490,000 | 1,010,000 |
| 5,000 / 90% | 4,500,000 | 630,000 | 1,870,000 |
| 5,000 / 100% | 5,000,000 | 700,000 | 2,300,000 |

- **Break-even sell-through.** With the prize and the 14% only, break-even is 38.8% at COP 6,000 and 46.5% at COP 5,000. Without the rights it is 33.3% and 40.0%.
- **Not included:** printing, design, seller commissions and any in-kind prize cost.
- **Bundle effect.** If 30%, 60% or 90% of tickets sell as "4 por 20.000", the average price is 5,700, 5,400 or 5,100. Gross at sell-out is then 5.7, 5.4 or 5.1 million. The pack saves the buyer COP 4,000.
- **Mini-raffle.** 300 tickets × 2,000 is 600,000 gross. The prize is 200,000 and the 14% rights are 84,000, leaving 316,000 net at 100% sold and 58,000 at 50%. The extra permit and workload argue for dropping it, or giving mini-raffle entries as a perk with main tickets [U].
- **Sell-out matters beyond vanity.** If the winner is the last 3 digits and numbers 000–999 are all in play, every unsold number is a chance of no winner. At 70% sold that is about 30% [U-math]. The bases must define the fallback.
- **Suggested ladder.** Price COP 6,000 for one ticket, COP 20,000 for four, with a cap of 10 per person. The cap also works as a quantity anchor (#20) [S].

### A5. Ethics

- **Age:** Sell to 18+ only (#39) [P]. Some first-year students may be under 18 [U].
- **Risk among students:** One Colombian health-student sample (n=243, Universidad del Cauca) found 5.8% possible gambling addiction and 2.5% some problems (#38) [P]. Don't target students in financial stress or people with debt [U].
- **Practical safeguards [U]:**
  - No credit or "fiado".
  - A per-person cap.
  - A refund policy if the raffle is cancelled.
  - A "juega solo con plata que te sobre" line.
  - No guilt or regret threats.
- **Local proverb:** "Aquel que juega por necesidad, siempre pierde por obligación" (#60) [S].

**Trust and distrust**
- **Scam anatomy.** Fake pages sell numbers by transfer, then vanish. Large groups, "winner" testimonials and organized structure fake legitimacy (#50, BBC 2017) [S].
- **2025 red flags.** Few followers or no history, prizes out of proportion, no clear rules or identifiable organizers, advance payments, artificial urgency (#50) [S].
- **Regulator pressure.** Expect "es ilegal" objections. The articles I read mention no exemption for small raffles (#48,#49) [S].
- **What builds trust (inverse of the red flags) [U]:**
  - Real name, face and local ties.
  - Permit number and written rules.
  - Official lottery and fixed date.
  - A public ledger.
  - A payee name matching Santiago's.
  - A live draw and a delivery video.
  - Prompt payout.
- **Reddit and forums.** r/Colombia and forum commentary could not be retrieved (search cap) [U].
- **Timing implication [U]:**
  - Sell on campus before Nov 28.
  - Use Dec 1–15 for family and adult networks.
  - A draw around Dec 14–19 avoids the Dec 24–31 clash with Christmas raffles.

### C2. Channel tactics

- **WhatsApp broadcast lists** hold up to 256 contacts each. They reach only people who saved your number, and replies arrive as private chats (#54) [S].
- **WhatsApp Status** lasts 24 hours and shows to your contacts. The FAQ snippet says Status video is limited to 30 seconds, which may be outdated (#54) [S].
- **WhatsApp Channels.** I did not open the FAQ. Their use here is optional, at most as a public record of draw results [U].
- **WhatsApp terms.** They prohibit bulk messaging, auto-messaging, auto-dialing and non-personal use unless authorized, and allow disabling accounts (#55) [P]. Use opt-in lists, personal 1:1 first, no bulk-send tools [U].
- **Group etiquette [U].**
  - Ask the admin first.
  - Post once and pin it.
  - Don't mass-tag or add people unasked.
- **Facebook, Instagram and TikTok.** Facebook and Instagram reach is in section B (#46) [S]. Meta's ad policy for raffles was not checked, so use organic posts only [U]. Short-video ideas are in D.

### C3. Does disclosing the cause help?

- **Tragedy framing is a weak bet.**
  - A meta-analysis finds the identifiable-victim effect small (r=.05). It appears mainly for single identified victims such as children, with monetary requests (#34) [S].
  - A preregistered replication of the classic study found no support (#34) [S].
  - In the one crowdfunding dataset I read, early family-and-friends support mattered more than narrative (#18) [S].
- **What to do instead [U].**
  - Disclose in two sentences: a used motorcycle for work.
  - Show how the money splits (prize, permit, costs, bike).
  - Add a photo of the target model.
  - Skip the sob story.
  - A raffle gives a real chance in exchange, so "te doy una oportunidad real y consigo mi herramienta de trabajo" is more honest than a plea.

### C5. Sellers, referrals, shops

- **Seller pay.** A 1% commission cut collected amounts, and 10% only recovered to 219.3 against 238.6 with no pay (#33) [P]. That sample was 16-year-olds collecting for charities [P].
- **Applied here [U].**
  - Unpaid helpers get recognition: board credit, a thank-you video, a small gift after the draw, not per-ticket pay.
  - Shop partners get a meaningful commission (10% or more) framed as a business deal.
  - Avoid token 1–2% commissions.
- **Who sells.** Landry et al. found solicitor attractiveness mattered about as much as the lottery mechanism (#26) [P]. Don't use appearance as a rule. Pick sellers with large, trusting networks [U].
- **"Trae a 3 amigos" [U].** Reward referrals in-kind and cap them in the bases.
- **Shops [U].**
  - A written agreement.
  - A numbered receipt book.
  - Weekly cash reconciliation.
  - A poster with QR and permit number.
  - Check whether sellers must be named in the authorization.

### C6. Honest urgency and gamification

- **Real counters.** A real "quedan X" counter updated on fixed times (for example 8 am and 8 pm). Say "los bonitos se van primero" only if the sales log shows it [U].
- **Early-bird draws.** Weekly early-bird mini-draws among paid tickets: I found no evidence. Art. 27 bans permanent raffles, so each draw may need its own authorization [U].
- **Safer engagement without extra prizes [U]:**
  - Milestone unlocks (at 500 sold, publish the bike photo).
  - "Número del día".
  - A seller leaderboard.
  - "Busca tu número": 0 plus your birth day (15 → 015), month plus day if the month has one digit (15 March → 315), the last 3 digits of your phone, or the digits of your motorcycle plate. Never the ID number.

### C7. KPIs and pacing

- **Weekly targets (U-shaped, my heuristic from #18).** Cumulative tickets by week: W1 300, W2 450, W3 550, W4 680, W5 830, W6 1,000. That is about 24 tickets a day on average [U].
- **Funnel math [U].** At 1.8 tickets per buyer there are about 556 buyers. At 15% ask→pay conversion that needs about 3,700 asks. Across 12 sellers over 6 weeks, that is about 51 asks per seller per week. If conversion is 8%, asks double, so decide by week 2 whether to extend.
- **Track:**
  - Tickets per day and cumulative %.
  - Asks, replies and payments by seller and channel.
  - Average tickets per buyer and the 4-pack share.
  - Unpaid reservations.
  - Response time under 10 minutes.
  - Complaints and refunds.
  - Board update timestamp.

### C8. Cases and failure modes

- **Chase the Ace, Inverness, Nova Scotia (2015).**
  - The jackpot grew weekly (revenue split 50/30/20) over 58 weeks, with about $5.8M in sales.
  - 13,000–15,000 visitors came in one week, and the final jackpot was $1.77M.
  - What drove it: a growing, ritualized public draw plus local pride and media.
  - It ended citing public safety and lack of facilities, and then spread (#57) [S].
  - Lesson: weekly rituals work, but any extra draws need legal clearance.
- **Potato Salad Kickstarter (2014).**
  - A $10 goal raised $55,492 over Jul 3–Aug 2, with 2,068 media mentions (#58) [S].
  - What drove it: deadpan humor and low stakes. The creator then held the promised party and funded a charity (#58) [S].
  - Lesson: humor works when paired with delivery.
- **Unauthorized raffle penalties.** The COP 116 million fine and five-year ban (#49) [S].
- **Fake raffles.** Organizers vanish after collecting payments (#50) [S].
- **Common failure modes [U].**
  - No permit.
  - No visible proof.
  - Sell-through short of target, leaving no winner or forcing refunds.
  - Cash leakage.
  - Double-sold numbers.
  - Pressuring contacts, which brings WhatsApp bans and reports.
  - A mid-campaign collapse.
  - Draw-day disputes over lottery, digits or date changes.
  - A late payout.
- **Not found:** I found no verified Colombian small-raffle success case [U].

## D. Language and humor

### D1. Register and expressions

- **Cundiboyacense register.** Interior Colombians extend "usted" beyond formality. "Sumercé" is a casual form, though mostly rural. "Bacano" and "chévere" are national (#59) [S]. Use "sumercé" sparingly as a playful nod [U].
- **Slang I believe is current [U].** "Luca" is 1,000 pesos, "palo" is a million, "de una", "pilas", "plata", "billete". Verify tone with your own circle.

### D3. Boundaries and humor evidence

- **Humor evidence.**
  - A meta-analysis of 369 correlations finds humor raises attention, ad liking and positive affect, and lowers source credibility. Its effect weakens down the persuasion chain (#36) [S].
  - Benign violation theory: humor needs a violation that is also benign, perceived at the same time (#37) [S].
  - Pair jokes with proof (permit, receipts) [U].
- **Avoid [U].**
  - Sexist, classist, ethnic or regional stereotype jokes (rolo, paisa, costeño).
  - Disability, religion and narco jokes.
  - Election politics.
  - Gambling-addiction jokes.
  - Mocking non-buyers.
  - Jokes about "no es estafa", which can raise suspicion.

## E. Visual identity

### E1. Gráfica popular (rótulos) and chiva art

- **Popular lettering.** It is handmade letters on urban surfaces, often with commercial marks, illustrations and ornament. Formal strategies: rigid commercial forms, free-hand cursive, diagonals, volume, perspective, shadows, highlights, bevels, dividing lines, asterisks and borders. Color separates sections and aids legibility (#62) [S]. Local terms include "letra con cachos" and "pechichona" (#62) [S].
- **Context.** Uniandes' "Letras hechas a mano" documents hand-painted signs in five cities: Bogotá, Buenaventura, Santa Marta, Barranquilla and Medellín (#63) [P].
- **Chivas.** Painted-bus art: I could not open a source, so I make no factual claims about it [U].
- **Typographic echo of rótulos.** Bungee was designed by David Jonathan Ross, inspired by urban signage (#66) [P/S]. Its Shade and Inline variants evoke shadowed sign lettering [U].

### E2. Lottery-ticket trust cues

- **Guilloché** is intricate mechanically engraved line patterns printed on banknotes, passports and certificates against forgery (#65) [S].
- **Ticket anatomy to borrow [U]:**
  - A stub (talón) and a perforation line.
  - A serial number.
  - A seal containing the resolution number.
  - A QR to the live board.
  - The lottery name and draw date.
  - An "+18, juego de azar" line.
- **Cautions [U].** Don't use official lottery logos or imitate security features in a way that implies officialness. Check Banco de la República's rules before reproducing banknote images; I did not verify them.

### E3. Color

## Key implications for this raffle

1. **Legal gate first.** Confirm authorization, whether a cash prize is allowed, which authority covers WhatsApp buyers outside Fusagasugá, and the 14% rights. Print the resolution number on everything (#39).
2. **One hero prize.** Keep the COP 2,000,000 prize as the single headline. Treat the mini-raffle as a perk or drop it (#26, #27).
3. **Price at COP 6,000, with a 4-pack at 20,000 and a cap of 10 per person.** Break-even sell-through is about 39% (6,000) or 47% (5,000) if the 14% applies. A 6,000 price with the pack weakly beats a flat 5,000 on gross. Price for logistics, not for rounded-number psychology (#21).
4. **Plan for sell-out.** With the last-3-digits rule, unsold numbers risk "no winner". Write the fallback in the bases.
5. **Let buyers choose, and publish the board.** A public 000–999 board gives choice, proof and scarcity at once. Say "no cambios" (#8, #9).
6. **Front-load, relaunch, close hard.** Close contacts in the first 72 hours, a second wave in weeks 3–4, a fixed sales close (#18).
7. **Use real scarcity only.** Pair the counter with proof (permit, public ledger, payee name), because scam guides flag urgency alone as a red flag (#50, #16).
8. **Make paying easy.** Take cash with numbered receipts and Bre-B/Nequi to one key in Santiago's name. Cash still dominates small payments (#43, #44).
9. **Time it to the calendar.** Sell on campus before Nov 28 (#41), and use Dec 1–15 for family networks. Don't count on the prima: 54.6% of employed Colombians are informal (#45, #40). Choose a fixed-weekday lottery and avoid Dec 24–31 (#52).
10. **Seller incentives.** No token commissions. Use recognition for helpers and 10% or more for business partners (#33).
11. **Tell the cause briefly.** Disclose the cause in two sentences with the budget split. Skip the tragedy framing (#34).
12. **Humor plus proof.** Humor raises attention but lowers perceived credibility. Pair every joke with a trust cue (#36).
13. **Ethics built in.** 18+ only, no credit, a per-person cap, a refund rule, and no regret or guilt threats (#39, #38).
14. **Design.** A rótulo-style headline with a guilloché-bordered ticket, a violet/yellow palette at 4.5:1 or better, and a board split into 10 images of 10×10 (#62, #65, #68).
15. **WhatsApp hygiene.** Personal 1:1 first, opt-in lists, no bulk tools, an opt-out line in every broadcast (#54, #55).
